Bringing human sciences into AI

My career in technology has made me curious about how our understanding of people shapes the technologies we build, and how those technologies, in turn, influence human behaviour. From psychology to neuroscience, scientific research has helped inform how we design and interact with technology. As AI advances, I'm interested in how these insights can help us better understand its effects on people and inform how AI systems are developed and evaluated.

Sara Portell
Founder, HCRAI

17 years inside technology companies

I spent over 17 years working in technology companies, including Shopify, Expedia Group and Unit4, leading international research teams and working across product, engineering and data science. I saw how research can shape digital products, and how the realities of human behaviour and organisational contexts matter just as much as the technology itself.

Closing the academic–practitioner gap

This led me to deepen my work in behavioural science, completing an MSc at the London School of Economics, followed by doctoral research in the psychology of emotions and wellbeing at Universidade Católica Portuguesa.

My research now examines human–AI interaction and the psychological effects of AI. Alongside this, through HCRAI, I work on AI evaluation and responsible governance, bringing behavioural and social sciences into the assessment of AI systems.

AI systems cannot be fully understood through technical performance alone.

I founded HCRAI to bring these perspectives together. We also need to examine how people interact with them, how they shape trust and decision-making, and where risks emerge in real-world contexts.

HCRAI applies human sciences to AI evaluation, product design and governance, translating evidence into practical recommendations that help organisations develop and deploy trustworthy AI systems.

Research collaborator

Yasmina El-Fassi

Yasmina is a PhD researcher in the Psychology of Emotions and Wellbeing at Universidade Católica Portuguesa, with a research focus on the psychological impact of empathic AI and human-AI interaction.

She holds a master's degree in Statistics and Information Management, bringing together expertise in data analysis, psychology, and user experience. Her research contributes to understanding how AI systems shape emotional experience, trust, and wellbeing in real-world use.

Alongside her academic work, Yasmina works as a consultant at a leading AI company focused on customer experience, contributing to the development of intelligent systems that support organisational insight and decision-making. She collaborates with HCRAI on selected research and advisory projects.
